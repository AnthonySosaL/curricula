Why most backtests lie

Test enough combinations and one of them will look profitable by pure luck. That's selection bias: the beautiful equity curve in your backtest is often optimized noise. ATLAS Lab tackles that problem head-on instead of hiding it.

How every strategy is validated

  • Double out-of-sample (18 months): the strategy is evaluated on data it never saw while it was being designed.
  • Deflated Sharpe Ratio: corrects the Sharpe ratio for the number of trials, so luck isn't rewarded. Read the explainer (Spanish).
  • Monte Carlo simulation: measures how fragile the result is to a different order of trades.
  • Real transaction costs: spreads and commissions included, not a frictionless world.
  • Preregistered methodology: the rules are fixed before looking at the results.

The (uncomfortable) conclusion

With data available to a retail trader, the lab did not find consistently exploitable alpha. Publishing that is the point: a well-measured negative result is worth more than a hundred screenshots of profits.

FAQ

Does ATLAS Lab sell signals or a bot?

No. It's an open research project; the data and conclusions are public and are not financial advice.

What is the Deflated Sharpe Ratio?

A version of the Sharpe ratio that discounts the effect of having tried many strategies. If you test thousands, it demands much more before calling a result real.

Can I see the negative results?

Yes, they're on the dashboard alongside everything else.
